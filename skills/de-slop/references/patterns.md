# De-slop pattern guide

Use this guide for a full audit, a long rewrite, or text that still feels synthetic after the basic pass. These are warning signs, not proof of AI authorship. Judge patterns by frequency, context, and effect.

## Content and structure

### Answer burial

Symptom: The useful answer appears after setup, praise, definitions, or a preview of the response.

Fix: Put the answer, decision, or next action first. Add context only where it changes interpretation.

### Premise stacking

Symptom: A paragraph explains the basis for a point and then asks or states the same point again.

Fix: Keep the clearest version. Move indispensable evidence after it.

### One-point dilution

Symptom: Several paragraphs restate one idea through synonyms, examples, and metaphors without adding substance.

Fix: Keep the strongest statement and the one best supporting detail.

### Fractal summaries

Symptom: The introduction previews the sections, each section recaps itself, and the conclusion repeats the whole piece.

Fix: Use only the orientation the reader needs. End after the final substantive point.

### Self-echo

Symptom: A distinctive word, phrase, or image keeps returning because the writer has run out of fresh substance.

Fix: Remove the echoes. Do not replace each one with a synonym unless the idea is genuinely new.

### Unnecessary balance

Symptom: Every criticism receives an automatic positive counterweight, or every benefit gets a generic challenges paragraph.

Fix: Include counterpoints only when evidence, fairness, or the user's decision requires them.

### Reasoning narration

Symptom: The prose describes what it is about to explain, what changed in the reasoning, or why it is being precise.

Fix: Present the conclusion and the relevant reasoning. Remove the commentary about composing it.

## Rhetoric and cadence

### Negative contrast

Common shapes:

```text
not just A, but B
it is not A; it is B
the issue is not A; the issue is B
no A, no B, just C
```

Why it fails: Repetition makes ordinary claims sound staged or falsely profound.

Fix: State B directly. Mention A only when the distinction prevents a real misunderstanding.

### Manufactured punch

Symptom: Several tiny sentences or fragments appear as separate paragraphs to force emphasis.

Fix: Join related thoughts. Reserve a short standalone sentence for a genuine turn.

### Self-asked questions

Symptom: The text asks a question and immediately answers it to create drama.

Fix: Write the answer as a normal sentence. Keep a question only when the reader must consider it.

### Rule-of-three repetition

Symptom: Adjectives, examples, clauses, or paragraphs repeatedly arrive in groups of three.

Fix: Use the number of items the subject actually requires. Vary list length naturally.

### Anaphora loops

Symptom: Three or more nearby sentences begin with the same words.

Fix: Keep repetition only when it creates deliberate rhythm and the passage can support it.

### False suspense

Watch for:

```text
Here is the thing
Here is the catch
Here is the kicker
What most people miss
The result?
The truth?
```

Fix: State the information without a drumroll.

### Quotable slogans

Symptom: A standalone line sounds designed for a slide or social post but carries little information.

Fix: Add the missing mechanism or cut the line.

### Forced figurative language

Symptom: A metaphor, simile, or personification adds mood without making the idea clearer.

Fix: Use the literal description. Keep imagery that is specific, apt, and consistent with the author's voice.

## Tone and diction

### Canned approval

Watch for praise or agreement before the answer:

```text
Great question
Excellent point
You are absolutely right
That is a smart observation
Certainly
Of course
```

Fix: Answer directly. Acknowledge emotion or achievement only when it matters to the exchange.

### Sycophancy

Symptom: The response adopts the user's claim, mood, or framing without checking it.

Fix: Separate courtesy from agreement. Correct errors plainly and support agreement with reasons.

### Inflated significance

Watch for unsupported claims involving:

```text
pivotal
transformative
groundbreaking
profound
crucial
vital
enduring legacy
turning point
reshapes everything
```

Fix: Name the measurable consequence or use a neutral description.

### Promotional tone

Watch for:

```text
seamless
powerful
dynamic
vibrant
innovative
best-in-class
unlock
elevate
supercharge
game-changing
```

Fix: Describe the feature, behavior, or benefit without selling it.

### Generic AI vocabulary

Treat these as density signals, not forbidden words:

```text
delve
tapestry
landscape
realm
intricate
interplay
nuanced
multifaceted
robust
meticulous
showcase
underscore
highlight
foster
leverage
harness
streamline
```

Fix: Replace only when the word is vague, repeated, inflated, or less natural than a simpler choice.

### Empty transitions

Watch for:

```text
Additionally
Furthermore
Moreover
It is worth noting
Importantly
Interestingly
Ultimately
In today's world
In an ever-changing environment
```

Fix: Delete the transition or express the logical relationship directly.

### Pompous substitutes for simple verbs

Symptom: The prose uses `serves as`, `stands as`, `represents`, `functions as`, `offers`, or `boasts` where `is` or `has` is clearer.

Fix: Use the simple verb unless the distinction is meaningful.

### Magic adverbs

Watch for unsupported emphasis from `quietly`, `deeply`, `fundamentally`, `remarkably`, `arguably`, or `meaningfully`.

Fix: Supply evidence or remove the adverb.

### Synonym cycling

Symptom: One object receives several names in one passage, such as product, platform, solution, experience, and ecosystem.

Fix: Choose one accurate term and repeat it when repetition improves clarity.

### Vague authority

Watch for claims attributed to unnamed experts, observers, critics, reports, or studies.

Fix: Name and cite the source, narrow the claim, or remove the attribution.

### False familiarity

Watch for `famously`, `notoriously`, `as everyone knows`, `classic`, or `widely recognized` without support.

Fix: Provide the evidence or drop the borrowed authority.

### Hedge piles

Symptom: Several uncertainty words cluster around one claim.

Fix: State the actual confidence and its basis once.

## Formatting

### Em dash use

Fix according to function:

- Interruption: use parentheses or split the sentence.
- Explanation: use a colon.
- Loose connection: use a comma if grammar allows.
- Strong turn: use a period.
- Range: use `to` or the correct range notation for the context.

Preserve the character inside exact quotations or source material when fidelity matters.

### Over-sectioning

Symptom: A short response has a title, overview, several tiny sections, key takeaways, and a conclusion.

Fix: Remove the title and merge related sections. A heading should help navigation, not decorate the page.

### Title-case headings

Fix: Use sentence case unless the product or publication style guide requires title case.

### Bold-first bullets

Symptom: Every bullet starts with a bold label and colon.

Fix: Use a plain list, a compact table when exact comparison matters, or paragraphs when the items are not parallel.

### Decorative markers

Symptom: Emojis, arrows, checkmarks, badges, and icons are used as routine bullets or section ornaments.

Fix: Remove them unless they encode status or meaning the reader needs.

### Gratuitous tables and cards

Symptom: A small amount of content is split into a comparison table or repeated cards with the same internal structure.

Fix: Use a sentence or list. Keep cards only when they support scanning, actions, or distinct objects.

## Interaction habits

### Generic closing offer

Watch for:

```text
If you would like, I can
Let me know if you want
Hope this helps
Feel free to ask
```

Fix: Stop. Offer a next step only when it is specific and useful.

### Fake collaboration

Symptom: The assistant says `we` or `our` in user-authored personal, professional, or creative material without a real shared role.

Fix: Preserve the author's point of view. Use `I`, the organization name, or a direct construction as appropriate.

### Unrequested tutorial voice

Symptom: Expert users receive elementary analogies, definitions, and step-by-step narration they did not request.

Fix: Match the reader's level. Explain only the parts needed to act or decide.

## UI copy pass

Check every visible string:

- Does the title say anything the surrounding page does not?
- Does the subtitle add information?
- Does helper text prevent a likely mistake?
- Does the button name the resulting action?
- Does an error identify the problem and recovery step?
- Does an empty state point to one useful next action?
- Are the same nouns and verbs used throughout the flow?
- Can any card, badge, label, or paragraph disappear without reducing understanding?

Remove any element whose only job is to make the interface feel busy, polished, or complete.
