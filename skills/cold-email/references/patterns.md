# Scenario patterns

> Note for teammates: the examples below were written for Ralston, so they use his AAPL and Datagrid/Procore background. Use your own why-me line from `MY_PROFILE.md`, or the team line, never his first-person line.


Same five-part skeleton every time: standing, them, you, gift, ask. What changes is
how much standing you have and how direct the ask can be. More standing buys a more
direct ask.

Examples are illustrative. Never ship one unedited.

---

## Contextual: same event, school, or employer

The strongest cold email that is still technically cold. Spend the shared reference
point in the first line and then get out of the way. Do not overclaim the connection.
"We were both at X" is honest. "Great meeting you at X" when you did not meet is a lie
they will catch.

```
Subject: aapl salt lake

Hi Seth,

We were both at the AAPL annual meeting in Salt Lake this summer.

You see something almost nobody else does. Cedar Ridge runs title and leasing for enough
operators that you watch the same paperwork arrive ten different ways, and you know
which of those ways costs people real time.

I built AI tools for construction companies at Datagrid and then Procore, and your
world looks like theirs from the outside.

I am talking to about twenty land groups this fall and will send you what I find
either way. Worth a conversation?

Sam
```

## Mutual connection

Lead with the name. It is the only thing that matters in the first line. Get
permission from the connector first, and say that you did, because "Dana suggested I
write" is checkable and they might check.

```
Subject: dana suggested i write

Hi Priya,

Dana Okafor said you are the person to talk to about how returns actually get
processed, and that you would tell me the truth about it.

Your team took on two more brands last quarter without adding headcount, which usually
means somebody is absorbing the difference manually.

I spent three years building this kind of tooling for mid-market retailers.

Is that a real problem for you or am I off?

Ben
```

## Research and learning outreach

No pitch at all. You want to understand something and they know it. The gift is the
synthesis, and it is genuinely valuable because nobody else is aggregating it. This
converts best when you have no product yet and should be honest about that.

The one obligation: actually send the writeup. It is the cheapest referral engine you
will ever have and failing to send it burns the list permanently.

```
Subject: how leases move

Hi Dana,

Eleven years of title work in the Williston means you have watched the county records
process change three times, and you know which change actually cost you time.

You would be one of about twenty people I am asking before I build anything, because
guessing at your work from the outside seems like a good way to get it wrong.

No pitch, and you would do most of the talking. Everything I learn comes back to you.

Want me to send it over?

Sam
```

## Hiring outreach

The candidate has ten of these in their inbox. Beat them by being specific about what
they built, not about how impressive your company is. Salary transparency early is a
real differentiator.

```
Subject: the migration post

Hi Sam,

Your writeup on cutting the Postgres migration from nine hours to forty minutes is the
clearest thing I have read on that problem all year.

We are four engineers with a similar scaling wall coming and nobody who has been
through it.

This may be nothing you want. But if it is, the range is 180 to 220 and I would rather
tell you that now than three calls in.

Worth a conversation?

Priya
```

## Investor outreach

The traction number is the whole email. If there is no number, wait. Warm intros beat
cold here by more than in any other category, so exhaust those first.

```
Subject: 4x since june

Hi Marcus,

You wrote last year that vertical AI would win in industries where the software is old
and the documents are worse.

That is the bet we are making in oil and gas land management. Revenue is 4x since June
across nine customers, all referred.

Not raising yet. Want me to keep you posted?

Sam
```

## Partnership or channel

The whole email is about their business, not yours. What do they already sell, and
where does it stop? You are proposing to make their thing more valuable.

```
Subject: where quorum stops

Hi Cody,

Your land module handles the record of truth well. The part your customers seem to
still do by hand is getting a scanned lease into it.

We do that piece for four operators now, all of them Quorum shops.

Worth a conversation about whether that helps you or annoys you?

Sam
```

---

## Re-engaging a dead thread

Same thread, never a new one. Give them the reason to re-enter without any guilt about
the silence. New information is the only legitimate excuse to reappear.

```
Hi Dana,

You mentioned in March that this would matter more once you closed the Anschutz deal.
That closed last month, so I thought I would check.

Still relevant, or has it solved itself?

Sam
```

---

## The break-up

Sent last. No guilt, no passive aggression, no "I'll assume you're not interested."
Written correctly, this gets more replies than any touch except the first, because it
removes the obligation to respond.

```
Hi Dana,

I will leave this here so I am not cluttering your inbox.

If document intake ever becomes the thing slowing you down, the door is open.

Good luck with the season.

Sam
```

## When you are writing to several people at one company

Give every person a different second paragraph. Colleagues forward emails. Identical
text across three inboxes reads as a mail merge and burns the whole company at once.

Better still: send to one person first. If they reply, go deeper with them rather than
emailing their colleagues cold. The second name at each company is follow-up ammunition.
