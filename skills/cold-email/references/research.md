# The data behind every rule

Gathered Sept 2026. Numbers move. The direction of each finding has held for years.

## Reply rates, for calibration

| Segment | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| Platform average 2026 | 3.43% (down from 5.1% in 2024) |
| "Good" | 8 to 12% |
| Signal-based, well-researched | 15 to 25% |
| Generic template | under 5% |
| Deep researched personalization | ~18% |

Inbox saturation and stricter spam enforcement drive the decline. Volume is not the
answer. Relevance is.

## Length

A study of 3 million cold emails: 50 to 125 words gets **8.2%** reply rate. Over 200
words drops to **3.9%**. That is a 2.4x difference for doing less work.

An 80-word email beats a 120-word email by about 15%. Lavender's analysis of 28.3
million sales emails puts the sweet spot even lower, at 25 to 50 words.

3 to 5 sentences: 3.6% average reply. 6 to 8 sentences: 2.4%.

Three short paragraphs is the structural target. Whitespace makes it scannable, and
most of these are read on a phone.

## Reading level

More than 70% of sales emails are written at a 10th grade level or higher. Writing at
a **3rd to 5th grade level gets 67% more replies**.

In practice this means short words, short sentences, and one idea per sentence. Executives are the most likely to read on a phone between meetings.

## Pronouns

Second person should outnumber first person **2 to 1**. Never open a first line with
yourself or your company.

This is the single most common failure. Most drafts run inverted, 1 to 3 the wrong
way, because the writer is thinking about their own credibility instead of the
reader's situation.

## The ask

Gong Labs studied **304,174 emails**. Asking for interest beat asking for a meeting by
a wide margin. Interest-based CTAs hit roughly 30% success, about twice any other type.

Mechanism: loss aversion. A reply costs seconds, a meeting costs thirty minutes. Sell
the conversation on the first touch, sell the calendar on the second.

Single-CTA emails get **35 to 42% higher response** than multi-CTA. Up to 371% higher
click-through.

## Subject lines

- 2 to 4 words: 46% open rate, the highest measured (analysis of 5.5M emails)
- 4 to 7 words (36 to 50 characters): 37% open, 22.3% response
- Personalized subject: 46% open vs 35% without
- Question-form subjects: 46% open
- Numbers in the subject: +113% open on average

Lowercase, specific, and boring beats clever. It should read like an email from a
colleague, not a campaign.

## Personalization

Reply rate goes from **3% with none to 7% with real personalization**. That is a 133%
lift. Martal puts it at 142% higher.

Only about 5% of senders personalize every email. This is the largest available edge
and it does not require any tooling.

## Links and attachments

A/B test: the no-link variant beat the linked version by **26%** on reply rate. The
goal of a first touch is a reply, not a click. Links also add spam-filter risk.

Attachments hurt deliverability directly. Spam filters flag them on size and file
type, and attachment-heavy campaigns underperform on both engagement and delivery.

## Timing

- Tuesday through Thursday, 8 to 11am in the recipient's local time zone
- Wednesday has the highest reply rate, Tuesday is the most reliable first touch
- Thursday 9 to 11am: 44% open rate

## Sequencing

- 58% of replies come from the first email. Follow-ups produce the other 42%
- 68% of positive replies come after touch two, 89% by touch five
- 4 to 6 touch sequences get about 3x a standalone send
- Spacing: 3 to 4 days. The 0 / 3 / 10 / 17 cadence captures roughly 93% of replies

## Deliverability floor

Bounces under 2%. Spam complaints under 0.3%. SPF, DKIM and DMARC authenticated, or
none of the above gets measured because nothing arrives.

Never send from the primary domain. A blacklisting takes the whole company's email
with it.
